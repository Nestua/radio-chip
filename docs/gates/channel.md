# Gate journal: channel simulator

Block: the simulated air between transmitter and receiver (R0-07). Model:
`crates/radio-phy/src/channel.rs`. Simulation only — nothing here becomes hardware, so it is
written in `f64` and has no G2/G3. Its job is to be a trustworthy test bench for the receiver
(R0-08…R0-10) and, later, for the drone link budget.

## 2026-11-17 · G0 · R0-07a
- Samples are complex `(f64, f64)` at `sample_rate_hz` (20 MHz for 802.11a/g, 5/10 MHz for the
  long-range profile).
- **RNG**: xoshiro256** seeded through SplitMix64 from a `u64`; one seed, one stream, on every
  platform. Normals by Box–Muller. Not cryptographic.
- **AWGN**: complex white Gaussian noise of power N = P / 10^(SNR/10), N/2 per I/Q axis, where P
  is the signal power the caller states (or `mean_power` of the slice). SNR is over the whole
  sampled band. For 802.11a/g with 52 of 64 bins occupied the per-subcarrier SNR is
  SNR + 10·log10(64/52) = SNR + 0.90 dB.
- **CFO**: x[n] · e^(j(2π·Δf·n/fs + φ0)). Transmit centre frequency and symbol clock are each
  ±20 ppm at 20/10 MHz and ±10 ppm at 5 MHz, from one oscillator (IEEE 802.11-2024 §17.3.9.5,
  §17.3.9.6); between two stations that is up to 40 ppm, 232 kHz at 5.8 GHz. `ppm_to_hz` converts.
  The matching sample-clock offset (SFO) comes with the fractional-delay filter in R0-07b.
- **Phase noise**: Wiener (free-running oscillator) model, φ[n] = φ[n−1] + N(0, 2πβ/fs), β the
  3 dB full linewidth. Its single-sideband level far from the carrier is L(f) = β/(2πf²), so a
  datasheet figure L(f0) dBc/Hz gives β = 2π·f0²·10^(L/10) (`linewidth_from_dbc`). Real
  synthesizers are flat inside the PLL loop bandwidth; the Wiener model is the worst case
  outside it and the usual first model (a PLL-shaped profile can follow when R3 hardware is known).

## 2026-11-17 · G0 · R0-07b
- **Fractional delay** (`fractional_delay`, also the engine of multipath and SFO): Kaiser-windowed
  sinc, 2·24 + 1 taps, β = 8; a band-limited signal is delayed by any real number of samples.
  802.11a/g occupies ±8.3 MHz of the ±10 MHz Nyquist band, which this filter must pass cleanly.
- **SFO** (`resample`): y[n] = x(n·(1 + ε)) with ε = ppm·1e-6 — a receiver clock fast by ε reads
  the transmitter's waveform at those instants. One oscillator drives carrier and symbol clock
  (IEEE 802.11-2024 §17.3.9.5–6), so a ppm offset means CFO = ppm·fc *and* SFO = ppm.
- **Fading taps**: a complex Gaussian process of unit mean power per tap, generated by shaping
  white noise in the frequency domain (power of each DFT bin = the exact integral of the Doppler
  spectrum over the bin, so the Jakes edge singularity is handled) and interpolating linearly
  from an update rate of 16× the highest Doppler frequency. Spectra: `Static`; `Bell` (TGn
  §4.7.1: S(f) = 1/(1 + 9·(f/fd)²), |f| ≤ 5·fd, fd = v₀/λ with v₀ = 1.2 km/h); `Jakes` (Clarke,
  mobile terminal: S(f) = 1/(π·fd·√(1 − (f/fd)²)), autocorrelation J0(2π·fd·τ)) for the drone.
- **Rician tap**: g = √(K/(K+1))·e^(j(2π·f_LOS·t + θ)) + √(1/(K+1))·scatter, mean power 1.
- **TGn models A–F** (IEEE 802.11-03/940r4, SISO): tap delays and per-cluster powers from
  Appendix C (`tools/ref/tgn_pdp_extract.py` → `tests/fixtures/tgn_pdp.txt`); clusters falling
  on the same tap add in linear power; total normalised to 1. LOS variants put the K-factor of
  Table II (B, C: 0 dB; D: 3 dB; E, F: 6 dB) on the first tap only. Model A is one flat tap.
  The MIMO angle parameters, the fluorescent-light and moving-vehicle Doppler components (§4.7.2–3)
  are not modelled (SISO, R0).

## 2026-11-18 · G0 + G1 · R0-07c front-end impairments
What a zero-IF receiver adds before the digital front end, needed to test R0-08 (DC blocker,
detector robustness, frozen CFO accumulators):
- **CW tone** (`add_tone`): x[n] + √P·e^(j(2π·f·n/fs + φ0)), P the tone's mean power. At 0 Hz
  it is a **DC offset** √P·e^(jφ0) (LO leakage, self-mixing); elsewhere a spur or interferer.
- **AGC gain step** (`gain_step`): samples from index `at` on scaled by 10^(g/20), instantaneous
  (the worst case for estimators that must be frozen across it). A DC offset that changes with
  gain is composed from both.
The air-to-ground channel moved to R0-07d.

## Open questions
1. RNG has no bit-level external reference (xoshiro256**'s reference outputs are not run here);
   its quality rests on the statistical checks and on every downstream curve matching theory.
   Acceptable for a simulator; revisit if a capture replay ever needs a shared stream.
2. Phase noise is Wiener only. For R3 hardware (AD9361) a PLL-shaped profile (flat inside the
   loop bandwidth) is more realistic; add it when the receiver's CPE tracking is tuned.
3. R0-07d: drone air-to-ground channel (two-ray ground reflection, elevation-dependent K, path
   loss) on top of the R0-07b machinery.
4. TGn models are SISO here; MIMO angles, the moving-vehicle spike of model F (§4.7.2) and the
   fluorescent-light modulation (§4.7.3) are not modelled.
5. TGn models cover delays up to 1050 ns (F) — beyond the 800 ns guard interval at 20 MHz; the
   long-range profile at 5/10 MHz has 1.6/3.2 µs guards. R0-09 should test E/F at 20 MHz as the
   ISI case.

## 2026-11-19 · `agc_transient`: gain, phase and DC states while the gain control settles

- **What:** sample n takes the first state with n < `until` (gain in dB, a carrier phase, a DC
  offset added after the gain); samples after the last state are unchanged. Used by
  `rxfront agc` (`docs/gates/rx_front.md`, 2026-11-19).
- **Reference:** its definition written out sample by sample, and the two older functions it
  generalises (both ours: this is a consistency gate of a simulator primitive, not an outside
  reference; `gain_step` and `add_tone` have theirs above): one state without phase or DC equals `gain_step` on the scaled signal, and a
  DC-only state equals `add_tone` at 0 Hz. Test `g1_channel_impairments::agc_transient_states`.
- **Mutations** (each fails the test): the rotation's sign; a state ending one sample late; the
  DC not added to the imaginary part.
- **Not modelled:** the settling time of a gain step (steps are instantaneous), a filter's
  transient after the step, and what a particular AGC does. The AD9361's fast-attack behaviour
  is to be taken from captures in R1.

## 2026-11-19 · G0 · R0-07e: a fast-attack gain control (`FastAttackAgc`)

**What it is for.** ADR 0007 puts two requirements on a real gain control: still from the end
of the short training field, and the packet inside a level window (−15 to −3 dBFS). Until now
the gain steps in `rxfront agc` were placed by hand. This model places them as a gain control
would: from the samples. It is simulation only (the gain control of R1 is the AD9361's own;
ours comes with R3).

**Reference: the published behaviour of the AD9361's fast-attack mode** (Analog Devices
UG-570 Rev. A, "Fast Attack AGC Mode", pp. 42–46, Fig. 25, Tables 20 and 23–26; register
reset values from UG-672 Rev. 0, the AD9364's register map; archive copies, read twice on
2026-11-19: by a research agent and, independently, by the pre-PR reviewer, who decoded the
reset values from the register table). What the documents state and the model takes over:

| Published | In the model |
|---|---|
| State 1, peak overload detect: an overload decreases the gain; the detectors are held in reset for the Peak Overload Wait Time after each change (reset 4 ClkRF); the state ends when no overload occurs for the Energy Detect Count (8 ClkRF) | `peak_wait`, `energy_detect`, in samples |
| Decrement by case (Table 20, reset values, gain indices of 1 dB): large ADC overload and (large LMT overload or digital saturation) 5; one of them 2; small ADC overload 2 | `step_both`, `step_large`, `step_small` |
| State 2: Settling Delay (20 ClkRF "based on 30.72 MHz", from the last change; the document's "Settling Delay minus Energy Detect Count" after State 1) then the average power over 16·2ⁿ samples (reset n = 5: **512**; "16 or more") and one gain change to the Lock Level (reset −10 dBFS, 1 dB steps); an overload returns to State 1 | `settling_delay`, `power_samples`, `lock_dbfs` |
| State 5, gain lock: the power measured at the lock is the reference; each time the power value updates (every 64 samples at reset) it is compared; a rise (stronger signal) or a fall (energy lost) by a threshold (10 dB each) that lasts twice the Gain Lock Exit Count (8) unlocks; so does a single large overload, if enabled | `lock_window` (updates in blocks counted from the lock), `stronger_db`, `lost_db`, `exit_count`, `unlock_on_overload` |
| Gain range 0 to about 73 dB at 2.3 GHz in 1 dB indices | `min_gain_db`, `max_gain_db` |

`FastAttackAgc::ad9361_reset(clocks per sample)` holds these reset values.

**Where the model departs from the documents or defines what they leave open** (each a
parameter or a stated simplification, to be replaced by R1's captures):
- One signal for all detectors. The chip has an analogue peak detector before its filter (LMT),
  one on the ADC's output and a digital saturation flag; the model compares the peak of the
  sample after the gain, max(|I|, |Q|) with 1.0 the ADC's full scale, with three thresholds
  (`both_peak` ≥ `large_peak` ≥ `small_peak`: 6 dB above full scale, full scale, 1 dB below:
  the model's own numbers). The split of the gain between the stages is not modelled.
- Exact sample timing: a decision at sample n acts from sample n + 1; the samples
  n + 1 … n + `peak_wait` are not examined; the quiet count runs from the change. After an
  unlock that changes the gain the detectors are not held off.
- The settling delay's reset value is used as 20 clocks whatever the clock; the document says
  it should scale with ClkRF (about 13 samples instead of 10 at 40 MHz: the reviewer's probe
  moved the latest change by 5 samples).
- The lock change is round(lock level − measured level) dB, limited to the gain range
  (the chip's limit on an increase, AGCLL Max Increase, reset 63, never binds here).
  0 dBFS is a mean |v|² of 1 (the convention of `rxfront`: a packet "at −15 dBFS").
- The reference in lock is the measured power moved by the lock change, not a new
  measurement. A lock on no signal at all is left by any power and never by less.
- On any unlock the gain goes to its maximum or stays (`unlock_to_max`). The document
  (Table 24) lets the stronger-signal and overload exits keep the index and only the lost
  signal return it; the gain idles at its maximum in every run here, so it makes no
  difference yet.
- **Whether a large overload unlocks at reset is not clear from the documents:** register
  0x110 resets with "don't unlock on a large overload" set, but UG-570's Table 26 makes that
  depend on a second bit (0x0FB[D6]) that resets clear, and its text calls the overload exit
  "recommended for a typical fast AGC configuration". `ad9361_reset` has it off; the
  measurements run both.
- States 3 and 4 (a 1 dB decrease on an overload after the lock change, before the final
  lock) and State 2A (an increase at low power) are not modelled: the lock is final at once.
  The 3 → 5 condition is not published.
- **Phase and DC at a gain change are not published at all** (the only statement found: the
  LNA's output turns by about 180° when it is bypassed, UG-672 register 0x022). They stay the
  assumptions of `agc_transient`: `AgcTrace::gain_states` hands the model's gain changes to
  it with a phase jump per change drawn from a stated range.
- Noise figure against gain, the filters' transients, the attack delay after entering
  receive: not modelled. A sample that is not a number counts as no signal.

**Gates (G1):** `tests/g1_agc.rs`. The state machine on inputs whose answer is worked by
hand from the table above (every change's sample and size); dB definitions (a constant
envelope locks within half a step of the lock level); then the two requirements of ADR 0007
measured on 802.11 packets over the input range.

- **With the reset values the requirement fails outright:** 512 samples of power outlast the
  preamble, the gain is still searching when the data symbols arrive and moves inside them
  in every frame.
- **"Still from the end of the STF" holds under two conditions that are settings, not
  defaults:** the power measured over at most 32 samples, and the lock left on a large
  overload. Then the latest change is at STF sample 88 (16 samples) to 118 (32). At 64
  samples 3 to 9 of 400 frames are late and the fine CFO estimate shows it (247 Hz at
  60 dB against 44).
- **Leaving the lock by the power alone loses frames** even with the short measurement: the
  power updates every 64 samples, so a strong packet clips for up to 72 samples before the
  gain moves; at 60 dB 45 of 400 frames miss exact timing. Not a question of the deadline
  (the latest change is at 152) but of what the detector sees before it.
- Every gain change of the working variants falls inside the coarse CFO window (STF samples
  32 to 144), which ADR 0007 gave up for this reason; the fine estimate's RMS is that of a
  clean channel at the same SNR.
- **The level window holds from 15 dB up; below it the gain is at its maximum and the packet
  lies at −30 dBFS + SNR.** That is not a failure of the control: the window's lower edge
  came from the fixed receiver's quantisation (`rx_front_level.txt`), and a packet 5 dB
  above the noise is limited by that noise: at −30 dBFS and 4 dB SNR that report shows
  1 % on the CFO error, and quantisation costs in earnest only from −45 dBFS down. The
  requirement is restated: inside −15 to −3 dBFS, or at the largest gain with the noise at
  or above the level the ADC needs (−65 dBFS, R0-08b). Where the noise sits at the largest
  gain is a property of the radio (R1).
- No gain change in the 400 samples of noise before any packet. After the lock, none in
  these packets (3 400 samples); the reviewer saw otherwise on longer ones with the overload
  exit on: 1000 octets at 6 Mb/s (27 000 samples) at 20 dB, 22 of 100 frames unlocked inside
  the packet on a peak of the data and 7 changed gain. The overload exit that the start of
  a packet needs is a hazard for the rest of it: the gain must be held once the packet is
  acquired (the chip has a pin for it; Analog Devices' tuning page recommends the same).
  A requirement on our own control (R3) and on R1's configuration.
- Analog Devices' own statements on 802.11a with this chip (the tuning page; a 2014
  EngineerZone answer) are a budget of about 3 µs for the lock, called hard to meet. The
  2 to 4.4 µs here (samples 38 to 88) are not a confirmation of anything: no measured lock
  time is published.
- Sensitivities probed by the reviewer on the first version (latest change, baseline 94):
  the noise at −45 or −20 dBFS 94 and 104; thresholds 3 dB lower 102; settling 13 samples 99.

## 2026-11-19 · G0 + G1 · R0-07e, second half: the DC offset per gain state (`GainDc`)

**What it is for.** The gain changes of `FastAttackAgc` carried no DC offset. A zero-IF
chain has one, it differs from gain state to gain state, and the receiver's DC blocker meets
the change inside the preamble. `GainDc` gives every stretch of constant gain its offset;
`AgcTrace::gain_states_dc` hands them to `agc_transient`, the settled stretch included (a
last state without end).

**Reference: what the AD9361's documents state about DC** (Analog Devices UG-570 Rev. A,
archive copy, read by a research agent on 2026-11-19; pages as it reported them):

| Published | In the model |
|---|---|
| "The RF DC offset correction for each enabled Rx gain index is stored in a LUT and applied when the Rx gain index is used." (p. 12); the baseband words likewise "applied as the Rx gain changes based on the current Rx gain table index" (p. 11) | What a calibrated chain leaves is a property of the gain index: `per_index`, one value per index of 1 dB, the same whenever the index returns |
| Tracking updates the RF correction on three events: "Rx gain change, no energy detected, or when the ENSM exits the Rx state" (p. 12), "only … at the current gain index" (p. 30); the baseband tracking is "a high-pass filter loop" with an option to "attack/settle quickly after a gain change" (p. 11) | Not modelled: the offsets are constant per index for the length of a run. The chip's own tracking is a second DC blocker in front of ours; R1's captures show what it leaves |
| **No level**: no residual in dBFS or dBc, no offset against gain index, no size or length of the transient at a gain step, no corner frequency of the tracking loop (UG-570, the register map, Analog Devices' "AD936x DC offset issue" note, the driver's documentation) | The levels are parameters, swept in the measurements |

**Defined here** (nothing published behind it):
- `per_index`: one magnitude at every index; the direction is drawn once per index from a
  seed, uniform. A real table's residuals differ in size from index to index.
- `before_gain`: an offset at the input of the gain, 10^(g/20) times that after it: the
  part nothing corrects (an uncalibrated chain, or one whose correction has aged). It is
  the case in which scaling the receiver's DC estimate by the gain step is exact; the
  per-index part is the case in which it is not.
- Both are added after the gain and before the ADC, in the units of the signal there. Each
  gain change still turns the phase by an assumed angle (`gain_states`).
- The register-level details of the tracking (event mask 0x18B, shifts 0x190 to 0x192) were
  read from a third-party mirror of UG-671 and are not used by the model; the mirror and
  the driver's documentation disagree on the order of the event bits. To be read from
  Analog Devices' own copy when R1 configures the chip.

**Gate (G1):** `cargo test -p radio-phy --test g1_agc` — 14 pass; the new one is
`dc_follows_the_gain_index_and_the_gain`: on a trace written by hand (gains 70, 60, 70,
60, 40 dB) the before-gain part is the input offset times 10^3.5, 10³ and 10²; the
per-index part has its magnitude at every state, the same value when an index returns,
another at another index and with another seed, whole-decibel indices; both parts add;
through `agc_transient` the settled stretch keeps its offset to the last sample; gains,
ends and phases equal `gain_states` with the same random stream; a trace without a change
is one state. It is the definition worked by hand, tied to the published statement in
the table's first row: a consistency gate of a simulator primitive, like `agc_transient`'s.
